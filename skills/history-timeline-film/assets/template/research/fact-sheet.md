## tablet · 約前 3300–3000 · 泥板與蘆葦筆

- **Claim:** In southern Mesopotamia people recorded goods by pressing a cut reed into wet clay; these proto-cuneiform tablets are mostly accounts.
- **Date / place / people:** c. 3300–3000 BCE; Uruk (southern Mesopotamia); anonymous scribes / administrators.
- **Visual facts:** palm-sized clay tablets, ruled cases, impressed (not carved) wedge marks; reed stylus with a triangular tip; sheep, grain, ziggurat-like temple platform as setting.
- **Facts for the info card:**
  1. Earliest proto-cuneiform tablets mostly come from Uruk, c. 3300–3100 BCE, and are administrative. — Wikipedia: Proto-cuneiform
  2. The wedge shape comes from the reed tip pressed at an angle. — Wikipedia: Cuneiform
  3. Cuneiform was used for about three millennia by several languages. — Wikipedia: Cuneiform
- **Sources:**
  - Wikipedia — Cuneiform — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform
  - Wikipedia — Proto-cuneiform — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-cuneiform
- **Do not claim:** that writing was "invented" in one city or year; that Sumer is the only origin of writing (Egypt is roughly contemporary); exact tablet counts.
- **Hedged wording used:** 「約」for dates; 「已知最早…大多出土於」instead of 「最早的文字」.
- **Strong words justified:** 最早 — always as 「已知最早的…」 for proto-cuneiform tablets from Uruk, as in the cited sources.

## quill · 約 6–19 世紀 · 鵝毛筆與手抄本

- **Claim:** From roughly the 6th to the 19th century the quill and iron-gall ink on parchment were Europe's main writing tools; monastic scriptoria copied and illuminated books.
- **Date / place / people:** c. 6th–19th c.; European monasteries; Isidore of Seville mentions quills in the early 7th c.
- **Visual facts:** goose flight feather, cut nib with slit, penknife, inkhorn, sloped writing desk, parchment ruled with lines, gold leaf and mineral blues/reds for initials.
- **Facts for the info card:**
  1. Isidore of Seville (early 7th c.) mentions writing with feathers. — Wikipedia: Quill
  2. Scribes re-cut nibs with a small knife; English "penknife". — Wikipedia: Quill
  3. Iron gall ink browns with age and is acidic. — Wikipedia: Iron gall ink
  4. Mass-produced steel nibs replaced quills in the 19th c. — Wikipedia: Quill
- **Sources:**
  - Wikipedia — Quill — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quill
  - Wikipedia — Iron gall ink — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_gall_ink
  - Wikipedia — Illuminated manuscript — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illuminated_manuscript
- **Do not claim:** that Isidore invented the quill; that all medieval writing was done in monasteries; a precise first year of quill use.
- **Hedged wording used:** 「已知最早的鵝毛筆記載之一」; 「約 6 世紀到 19 世紀」.
- **Strong words justified:** 最早 — only as 「已知最早的…記載之一」 (Isidore of Seville's mention of quills), as in the cited sources.

## ballpoint · 1938 · 原子筆

- **Claim:** In 1938 László Bíró and his brother György filed a British patent for a ball-tip pen with quick-drying viscous ink; they patented and produced it in Argentina in 1943.
- **Date / place / people:** 1938 (UK filing), 1943 (Argentina patent + production); László and György Bíró.
- **Visual facts:** tiny metal ball in a socket at the tip, ink tube, thick oil-based ink; mid-century poster look (flat inks, bold sans type).
- **Facts for the info card:**
  1. John J. Loud patented a ball-tip marker in 1888 (for leather; too coarse for paper). — Wikipedia: Ballpoint pen
  2. Bíró brothers: British patent filing 1938, Argentine patent and production 1943. — Wikipedia: László Bíró
  3. The RAF adopted the pen because it did not leak at altitude like fountain pens. — Wikipedia: Ballpoint pen
  4. Ink flows by gravity, so most ballpoints stop writing upside down. — Wikipedia: Ballpoint pen
- **Sources:**
  - Wikipedia — Ballpoint pen — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballpoint_pen
  - Wikipedia — László Bíró — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_B%C3%ADr%C3%B3
- **Do not claim:** that Bíró invented the first ballpoint (Loud 1888 came first); brand names; exact sales figures.
- **Hedged wording used:** 「申請專利」rather than 「發明」; 「最普及的書寫工具之一」style wording if exact data is missing.
